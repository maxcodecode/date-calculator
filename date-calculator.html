Who uses this calculator

Most people use this tool because a document gives them a number of days, not a date. They have a starting point in hand — a signature date, a procedure date, a project kickoff — and they need to know where the deadline actually lands on the calendar. Here are the situations that come up most often.

Contracts and legal deadlines

A contract says delivery must happen within 90 days of signing. The signing date is March 15. You need to know the exact delivery deadline. Counting from today would give the wrong date — you need to count from the signing date.

Medical and pregnancy timelines

A doctor schedules a follow-up 280 days after the first day of the last menstrual period. That first day is a fixed date in the past, not today. You need the exact date of the follow-up visit.

Project milestones

A project kicks off on a specific date. The first milestone is due "100 days after kickoff". You need the calendar date, not the number of days, to schedule reviews and align the team.

Visa and immigration windows

A visa is approved on a certain date and is valid for 100 days from approval. You need to know the last day you can enter, not the number of days remaining.

Course and exam schedules

A course starts on a fixed date, and the exam is scheduled 100 days after the start. You need the exam date to plan revision and book time off.

Billing and payment terms

An invoice is issued on a specific date and payment is due 30 days later. You need the due date to avoid late fees, not the number of days left.

Audit and historical review

An audit or legal review asks: "What date was 100 days after January 1, 2024?" You are working backwards from a historical starting point. Today's date is irrelevant.

FAQ

How does the date calculator work?

Enter a number of days. The tool adds that number to today's date, or to a start date you choose, accounting for leap years and month lengths.

Can I calculate a date in the past?

Yes. Enter a negative number to see how many days before today.

Can I calculate from a specific date instead of today?

Yes. Change the start date in the input above. The result will update automatically. A progress bar will appear showing how much time has passed and how much is left.

Does it use my local date and time zone?

Yes. The calculation uses your device's local date and time zone.

What is the difference between calendar days and business days?

This tool counts calendar days, including weekends and holidays. If you need business days only, you need to subtract weekends manually.

Does it handle leap years?

Yes. The calculation uses JavaScript's built-in Date object, which automatically accounts for leap years, including February 29.

Why does the result sometimes differ by one day from what I expected?

This usually happens when the starting date is counted differently. Some people count today as day 1. This tool treats today as day 0 and adds the full number of days.
